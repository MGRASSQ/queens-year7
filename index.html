Screens

How much of Year 7 learning should be on a screen?

Background
  • Queen's asks families to provide a learning device.
  • Schools differ: Highgate teaches mostly on paper; Putney says devices aren't essential for homework.
Read more & resources

At Queen's

  • Queen's asks families to provide a learning device for Year 7.
  • We couldn't find a published figure for how much Year 7 work needs a screen, at Queen's or any other school.

Other London schools (18 compared)

  • Highgate: paper-based lessons, no routine one-device-per-pupil scheme.
  • Putney High: tablets and laptops are not essential for homework.
  • Channing: a mix of paper and digital, with Year 7 homework time guidance.
  • South Hampstead: a one-to-one iPad programme, with screen-use guidance for families.

Wider evidence

  • UNESCO's 2023 global education report found little robust evidence that digital technology adds value to learning, and that benefits fade when it is overused.
  • In 2024 the House of Commons Education Committee reported on screen time's impact on children's education and wellbeing.

Phones

Are the Yondr pouches working as intended?

Background
  • Queen's uses Yondr pouches, as do 10 of 18 London schools we compared.
  • Some parents have heard pupils can still use phones in them.
Read more & resources

At Queen's

  • Pupils lock phones in Yondr pouches in front of staff; pupils who break the rules are sanctioned.
  • Some parents have heard that pupils can still use phones in the pouches.
  • On one trip, pupils were asked to unlock phones to save a contact number. The school says it will review this.

Other London schools (18 compared)

  • 10 use pouches, 4 ban smartphones, 2 keep phones off in bags, 1 uses lockers, 1 discourages smartphones.
  • South Hampstead, Channing and St Paul's Boys' publish daily checks, sanctions and measures against workarounds.

National picture

  • Since September 2026, statutory government guidance expects schools in England to be phone-free by default.
  • In November 2026 the government said it will go further: phones out of reach all day, including breaks and lunch, from September 2027.
  • Schools reporting secure phone storage rose from 9% (May 2025) to 21% (June 2026).
  • OECD research: pupils distracted by others' devices in maths lessons scored lower in maths tests.

Social media

Should the school say more to families about app age limits?

Background
  • WhatsApp and most social apps have a minimum age of 13.
  • The school acknowledges the limits and is considering a reminder to Year 7 parents.
Read more & resources

At Queen's

  • The school acknowledges app age limits and agrees pupils would be best placed to follow them.
  • A reminder to Year 7 parents is being considered.
  • A Childnet online-safety talk for pupils, and a webinar for parents, are planned for 7 January.

The age limits

  • WhatsApp's minimum age in the UK is 13 (lowered from 16 in April 2024). Most social apps are 13+.
  • In June 2026 the government announced a ban on social media for under-16s, expected from spring 2027. Messaging apps like WhatsApp are not intended to be covered.
  • In the government's consultation, 9 in 10 parents backed an under-16 ban.

Other London schools

  • South Hampstead: no social media under 16.
  • Highgate: explicitly supports delaying social media.
  • Putney High: supports delay, with guidance for parents.
  • Channing: social apps blocked on school devices.
